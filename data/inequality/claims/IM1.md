When the after-tax return on capital exceeds the growth rate of the broader economy — the r > g condition — wealth held in capital compounds faster than labor income, and accumulated capital flows disproportionately to households who already hold it. The empirical persistence of this gap since 1980 in major economies provides a mechanical driver of widening top-share concentration that is independent of any specific policy choice.
