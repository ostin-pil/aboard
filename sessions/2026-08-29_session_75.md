## What happened

### 1. The disclosure was right, its placement was not

The recommendation was to keep the disclosure and change two things about
it, and that is what was applied.

It had been bolted onto the paragraph that argues the design is sound,
which is the persuasive core of the post. That paragraph went to 203 words
against a next-longest of 119, making it a structural outlier in a post
whose other paragraphs run 33 to 119. The tell was measurable rather than a
matter of taste. It now sits with the invitation, which is where a
contributor is actually deciding whether to file something and therefore
where an expectation about review latency belongs.

The borrowed comparison was cut entirely. Quoting `awesome-mcp-servers` at
3,575 open pull requests against 74 merged in thirty days conceded a
problem this project does not have: that list takes roughly sixty
submissions a day because anyone can add a line, aboard has 25 claims and
no inbound volume, and one post does not close that gap. Presenting it as
"the pressure this post creates" was borrowed anxiety, and a reader who
checked the analogy would have found it doing work the evidence does not
support. What survives is the true and useful part, that review is one
person and a queue of one is fast today, plus a pointer to
`review-capacity.md` for anyone who wants the measurement.

The telemetry evidence was also split into its own paragraph. It had been
sharing one with the invitation while making a different kind of point:
evidence of existing traction rather than an instruction to the reader.
Longest paragraph is now 112 words and the spread is 33 to 112.

### 2. The timing advice was backwards

Session 74 closed by suggesting the operator check when the next FutureEval
season opens, framing the risk as "landing in a quiet week". That framing
implied the goal is to land during an active season. The record says the
opposite, and so does the project's own plan: `plans/agent-distribution.md`
asks to post "before the next FutureEval season opens, so the audience is
looking for tooling", and its decisions section says to aim before the next
season announcement.

Measured against what Metaculus has published:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Summer 2026 announced | 2026-05-01 |
| Summer 2026 opened | 2026-05-18, a seventeen-day lead |
| Summer questions stop | "a few weeks before September 1, 2026", so already |
| Seasons per year | three (Fall, Spring, Summer), roughly $50k each |
| Fall 2026 | no announcement findable |

So today sits in exactly the gap the plan was aiming for. Summer is winding
down, Fall is unannounced, and bot builders are between seasons deciding
what to change, which is when someone is receptive to a new tool rather
than committed to a stack.

The inference worth flagging as soft: an early-to-mid September Fall
announcement is extrapolated from a single observed interval, Summer's
seventeen days, and is not a published date. It is a prompt to move, not a
deadline. The case to avoid is posting after the Fall announcement, which
would compete with it for the same readers.

## Decisions worth remembering

- **A caveat in the wrong section is a different sentence.** The
  disclosure's content was never the problem; putting it inside the
  argument for the design turned it into a hedge on that argument. Moving
  it to the invitation made the same words read as an expectation being
  set rather than a confidence being withdrawn.
- **Paragraph length was the measurable tell.** 203 words against a
  next-longest of 119 said something was bolted on. Worth reaching for
  when prose feels wrong but the reason is not obvious.
- **Do not borrow someone else's failure numbers.** The
  `awesome-mcp-servers` backlog is real and the note that measured it is
  sound, but importing it as this project's incoming pressure asserted a
  volume nothing predicts. Honesty about a limit does not require
  overstating it.
- **Advice given without checking is worth checking.** Session 74
  recommended researching the FutureEval window and, in the same breath,
  characterised the stakes wrongly. The plan had already recorded the
  correct framing, so the answer was one grep from home and the research
  only confirmed the dates.
- **A dependency's merge can invalidate a measurement.** Session 72
  landing mid-discussion could have moved what `mcp_call` counts. It did
  not, but the baseline was only trustworthy after looking.
