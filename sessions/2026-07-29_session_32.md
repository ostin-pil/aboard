## What happened

### 1. Where the external landscape bends the plans

Three checked facts reshaped the growth thinking, each recorded with its
source in the new plan files:

- Agent traffic passed human traffic on the web this year, and it fetches
  HTML. `llms.txt` is essentially unread (an Ahrefs server-log study found
  97% of the files got zero AI-bot requests in May 2026). The entity-page
  bet in `research/agent-first-validation.md` is confirmed from outside;
  the claim page itself is the agent surface.
- MCP discovery genuinely runs through registries (mcp.so, Smithery, Glama,
  awesome-mcp-servers), which makes verifying session 30's unconfirmed
  registry publication the cheapest distribution lever available.
- News-seeded forecasting-question generation is a proven technique
  (FutureSearch; Bosse et al. 2026: 96% well-formed questions, 95%
  auto-resolution), and no product occupies news-to-mechanism mapping.

The strategic frame came from `funding-applications.md`: in this category
the evidence bar is consumption, so traffic work and funding work are the
same work.

### 3. The signals track: the operator's vision as ring architecture

The original vision was agent swarms filing issues, proofs, and solutions
on something that behaves like a social platform, with a news layer on
top. Both survive the project's recorded constraints only as a layer
outside the canonical graph, which the operator framed as onion
architecture. That became three plans:

- `plans/signals-substrate.md`: ring 0 is the canonical graph, unchanged,
  never referencing outward; ring 1 holds signals (news links,
  annotations, endorsements) written by OAuth-identified agents without a
  PR, stored in D1 behind the Worker, referencing ring 0 by IRI, with a
  boundary lint for orphans, a kill switch, and promotion through the
  existing proposals path as the only inward door.
- `plans/news-layer.md`: attach (news signal onto an existing claim, no
  review burned), seed (short-horizon forecast through the full PR toll,
  `resolutionSource` from the story), promote (durable links become
  canonical edges), sweep (a daily job tuned to single-digit seeds per
  week). The graph is the filter that keeps this from becoming an
  aggregator, and news seeding is what manufactures the short-horizon
  slate the flywheel has been missing.
- `plans/agent-social-layer.md`: likes become typed endorsements
  aggregated diversity-first (facets in v1, bridging when contributor
  diversity supports it; raw counts never shown alone; endorsements rank
  attention and never resolve anything), comments become typed annotations
  (question, objection, evidence pointer) on a talk-page tier, and
  per-agent contributor pages carry the status economy. The OAuth slice
  session 31 shipped is the account system this layer spends.

## Decisions worth remembering

- **Consumption is the evidence bar**, so growth work is funding work.
  The external-consumer item and the first resolved forecast remain the
  two deep bottlenecks; nearly everything planned this session serves one
  of them.
- **Stop expecting anything from `llms.txt`.** Keep it because it is
  free; invest in the claim pages, the registries, and the feeds, which
  is where agents demonstrably are.
- **The ring model is the only version of social and news mechanics
  consistent with the project's research.** Raw likes are a Sybil
  target, free text is slop, and PR review cannot absorb news velocity;
  layering solves all three at once and makes the promotion gate the
  editorial identity expressed as architecture.
- **State an architecture once.** The ring model lives in
  `signals-substrate.md` and the feature plans reference it; two copies
  would drift.
- **Court the FutureEval community as consumers, not competitors.** The
  accuracy axis stays rejected; the complement (curated questions bound
  to causal structure) is unoccupied.
- **A parallel session's merge is a mid-session event.** Merging
  `origin/main` into the docs branch and de-staling the index here was
  cheaper than leaving the conflict to the PR.
