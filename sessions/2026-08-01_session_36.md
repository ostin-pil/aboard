## What happened

### 1. The short-horizon slate, and the calendar problem

§3 asks for questions resolving by 2027-03. Drafting them against the plan's
own suggested sources showed that most of them cannot deliver inside that
window. V-Dem released v16 on roughly 2026-03-16 and Freedom House released
FIW 2026 on 2026-03-23, both covering the prior calendar year. A question
resolving "by 2027-03-31" against either is a coin flip on the publisher's
calendar, which is precisely the unresolvability §3 exists to remove. The
Reuters Institute Digital News Report publishes in June and is simply outside
the window.

The fix was to prefer series with monthly, quarterly or continuous cadence,
and it produced six forecasts rather than the planned three to five:

| ID | Claim | Resolves | Anchor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IF2 | IM1 | 2027-02-28 | BLS labor share, Q4 2026 against Q4 2025 |
| IF3 | IM2 | 2027-03-31 | Census BPS, 2026 permits at least 1% over 2025 |
| F6 | L3 | 2027-03-31 | OECD.AI binding-initiative count above 151 |
| F7 | M4 | 2027-03-31 | DSA automation share above 50% |
| F8 | L3 | 2027-03-31 | DSA Article 42 reports, 5 of 6 platforms |
| F9 | S1 | 2027-01-31 | CIVICUS Repressed-or-Closed above 83 |

IM1 and S1 had no forecast at all before this, and both carry four edges, so
they were the largest coverage gaps in the graph.

The democratic-backsliding domain was nearly left without short-horizon
coverage for the calendar reason above. CIVICUS rescued it, but not for the
reason the session first proposed. Its Watchlist is unusable here: no
published cadence, and a watchlist entry is a researcher assessment rather
than a measurement, which is close to the says-trigger pattern the lint
targets. What makes CIVICUS work is that its annual ratings ship with the
People Power Under Attack report in early December, three months clear inside
the window, where V-Dem and Freedom House sit on the boundary.

F6 and F8 replace F5, F7 replaces F4. The originals stay filed. Editing
`resolutionCriteria` on a forecast that already carries predictions changes
what those predictions were answering, so the repair is a new forecast rather
than an edit, and this is now recorded in `plans/integrity-foundations.md`.

Every baseline in the criteria was read off the source on the day of filing:
151 binding of 2457 initiatives, 43% automation share, 83 of 198 rated
Repressed or Closed, 54.1% labor share. One nearly went wrong. A search
summary offered a CIVICUS per-category breakdown summing to 197 countries and
implying 77 in the two worst bands; the official press release says 83. The
number came from the primary source instead.

F9 is the first forecast in the corpus that can resolve **annulled**, using
the `null` outcome arm session 34 shipped as a reserved shape. If CIVICUS has
not published by 2027-01-31 the question is unresolvable rather than pending,
and the criteria say so.

### 2. The ensemble, and what it was writing into `data/`

The first dry run against IF1 emitted citations including a DOI and an
`nyc.gov` path that looked plausible and were not. Checking what earlier runs
had already committed turned this from a caught mistake into a finding: of 56
distinct URLs in the `baseRates` and `dataAnchors` blocks of F1 to F5, **20
were dead**, including `doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1806880116` returning 404 (an
invented DOI), a 404 Pew slug reading "partisan-divisions-continue-to-groove",
a Gelman paper hosted at a UCLA address he has no affiliation with, and a
nonexistent `algorithmicauditing.org`. All of it was live in the published
JSON-LD, against CLAUDE.md's rule that every URL in `data/` be a real landing
page.

`ensemble-predict.ts` gained `--no-model-sources`, which drops model-authored
citations and keeps probability, reasoning and attribution. This run added
zero new URLs to `data/`, verified by diffing.

Two of the four configured Groq models had been retired upstream and returned
404. Querying the live catalogue gave `qwen/qwen3.6-27b` as a replacement for
the dead `qwen3-32b`, leaving three distinct model families. Groq's current
catalogue has no fourth capable family that is not a same-family sibling, so
the fourth slot was left empty rather than filled with a weaker duplicate that
would add noise to the median without adding independence.

18 predictions landed across seven forecasts. The most interesting result is
F7, which now carries the widest spread in the corpus at 0.46: the seed sits
at 0.12 and both models near 0.57. The models read 43% as within reach of 50%
without noticing that most of calendar 2026 is already recorded at 43%, and
one asserted a five-to-six-point annual trend the database is too young to
support. The seed was left alone. Disagreement is the output.

`content/about.md` refused to build until every forecast with two or more
predictions had a spread reading, which is session 29's design working exactly
as intended: the corpus cannot grow without the published interpretation
keeping pace.

### 3. Two dossiers

IM2 (housing supply) and L3 (platform antitrust and algorithmic transparency),
four ranked cruxes each. Given what the audit had just turned up, every source
was verified before writing: each DOI was resolved through content
negotiation and its returned title, authors and year checked against the
citation. All six matched.

L3's con side turned out to rest on unusually direct evidence. The 2020
Facebook experiments (Guess et al. in Science, Nyhan et al. in Nature) moved
users to a chronological feed for three months and found no detectable change
in polarization, knowledge or turnout. That is evidence against the mechanism
L3 asserts, which is consistent with the claim's own confidence of 0.45. Its
top-ranked crux is whether an experiment of that shape could detect the effect
at all, which is the honest terminal state for that debate rather than a
verdict either way.

Both dossiers name what each crux would operationally change, including two
where the answer is that resolution would change little.

Writing them surfaced a YAML trap worth remembering. A plain scalar cannot
contain a colon followed by a space, so prose like "productivity is highest:
Hsieh and Moretti" parses as a nested mapping and fails. The long prose fields
use folded block scalars now.

### 4. The citation cleanup

Widening the audit past F1 to F5 gave the real picture: 56 model-authored URLs
with 20 dead, and 81 curated URLs with 8 dead.

For the model-authored blocks the fix was removal, not repair. The `finding`
text on each citation is model-authored too, so a live URL paired with an
invented summary of what it shows reads as more rigorous than no citation at
all; keeping the live half would have preserved exactly the misleading
records. 41 blocks were removed line-based rather than by YAML round-trip, and
all five files were then re-parsed and diffed field by field against HEAD to
prove nothing else changed.

The eight curated dead links were link rot rather than fabrication and were
repaired: Freedom House and Chetty moved paths, Piketty's PSE host stopped
resolving (both citations now point at Harvard University Press),
`bowlingalone.com` serves a self-signed certificate, and the Boxell,
Gentzkow and Shapiro paper has since appeared in Review of Economics and
Statistics so it is cited by DOI with the corrected year.

One could not be repaired as written. The Brookings "case for a stronger
estate tax" article does not exist. This one was seed-authored, not from the
ensemble, which is worth noting: the failure mode is not unique to the
open-weights models. It is replaced with a real Brookings piece on expanded
estate-tax designs, and its `finding` rewritten, because the original claimed
a tax increase would produce revenue loss.

All 83 curated URLs now resolve. Three return 403 or time out to automated
clients (FRED, Simon & Schuster, the REStat DOI); FRED was confirmed live
through a real browser and the DOIs through content negotiation, so those are
bot-walls rather than dead links.

## Decisions worth remembering

- **Check when a source publishes, not just that it exists.** Half the
  plan's suggested anchors release in late March and cannot settle a
  question due 2027-03-31. Cadence is part of whether a forecast is
  resolvable, and it is invisible until you look.
- **A live URL with an invented finding is worse than no citation.** It is
  why the F1 to F5 cleanup removed whole blocks rather than pruning dead
  links: the surviving half would have looked verified.
- **Fabricated citations are not an open-weights problem.** The one
  irreparable link was seed-authored by Claude. The tooling fix helps going
  forward; it does not cover what is already committed.
- **Do not pad an ensemble to hit a count.** Three distinct model families
  beat four where the fourth is a weaker sibling, because the median gains
  noise without gaining independence.
- **Model-authored `finding` text is the part nobody checks.** URL liveness
  is easy to verify and gives false comfort; what the source actually says is
  the claim being made.
- **The about-page gate is load-bearing.** It blocked the build until every
  new forecast had a published spread reading, which is the correct coupling
  between corpus and interpretation.
