## What happened

### 1. The external resolution anchor on `Forecast`

`resolutionSource` reuses the existing `Source` type, per the plan's
recommendation, so it is already validated, serialized and JSON-LD mapped.
It names the third-party dataset or publication a reader checks to settle
the question, which is the one field that puts resolution outside the agent
graph.

`resolvedOutcome` and `resolvedAt` carry the result once it lands. The plan
specified the union as `"yes" | "no" | number | null`; building it surfaced
why the `null` arm matters, and it is not the same thing as the field being
absent. Absent means the forecast is not resolved yet. An explicit `null`
means it resolved as annulled, so it is excluded from scoring rather than
sitting pending forever. Without that distinction, a question that turns out
to be unresolvable quietly corrupts any future scoring denominator. A Zod
refine keeps `resolvedAt` and `resolvedOutcome` travelling together, since a
resolution with no date cannot be audited in order.

The numeric arm is reserved rather than observed: no `Prediction` shape can
express a range forecast yet, and the schema doc says so rather than
implying the capability exists.

Rippled in the same commit, per the CLAUDE.md rule: `public/schema/v0.json`,
`src/lib/jsonld.ts`, `research/schema.md`.

### 2. `Edge.rationale` became required

The plan recorded 19 existing edges, all carrying a rationale. The real
count is 25, because a third domain (`epistack_cases`) landed after the plan
was written. All 25 carry one, so the change costs nothing today and closes
the gap it existed to leave open. Relations are meant to be classified on
stated reasoning, and a count-based reading of the graph is what a collusion
attack optimises against.

### 3. The resolution lint

Three heuristics over resolution prose: criteria that resolve on somebody
saying something, criteria with no checkable threshold, and forecasts with
no external source at all.

The rules live in `src/lib/resolution-lint.ts` as pure functions, with the
filesystem half in `scripts/lint-resolution.ts` and 19 unit tests in
`src/lib/resolution-lint.test.ts`. The plan's verification step called for
adding a deliberately bad fixture by hand, confirming it was caught, and
removing it. Vitest fixtures do the same job durably, and the repo's test
include glob is `src/**/*.test.ts`, which is why the pure half sits in
`src/lib/` rather than in `scripts/`.

Warn-only and outside the build, deliberately. These are prose heuristics
over a corpus that predates the anchor field, and failing `npm run build` on
them would be the wrong trade. `--strict` exists for when the corpus reports
clean.

The `says-trigger` rule deliberately excludes "claims that". This is a
claims graph; the word appears in ordinary criteria prose too often to carry
signal. Tests pin that, along with "United States" not tripping the
`states? that` pattern.

### 4. The backfill, and the two forecasts it could not fix

Five of six forecasts gained a `resolutionSource`, each URL fetched or
confirmed against a search index rather than recalled: ANES for F1, the OECD
Well-being Data Monitor for F2, the Reuters Institute Digital News Report
for F3, the OECD.AI Policy Navigator for F5, and the Census Building Permits
Survey for IF1. Findings went from 7 across 6 forecasts to 2 across 2.

The two that remain are real, and both were left flagged on purpose.

**F4 (`no-source`)** asks whether a major platform will voluntarily publish
ranking parameters. No registry tracks voluntary first-party disclosures, so
there is no landing page to cite. Naming one would fabricate the very anchor
the field exists to make honest. The defect is question design, not a
missing URL.

**F5 (`no-threshold`)** turns on whether a statute is "binding" and
"distinct from DSA requirements", which is a judgement boundary rather than
a number. The lint is being blunt here, since a statute passing is a genuine
binary event, but the boundary really is unstated.

Both are fixable only by rewriting `resolutionCriteria`, and both forecasts
already carry filed predictions. Editing the question after agents have
predicted against it changes what they were predicting and silently
invalidates the record, which is the same class of move the anti-gaming
research exists to rule out. Adding `resolutionSource` to the other five was
legitimate precisely because it adds an anchor without altering the
question. The repair for F4 and F5 is a new, well-specified forecast
alongside the old one, which belongs to `corpus-growth.md` §3.

## Decisions worth remembering

- **Absent and `null` are different resolution states.** Not resolved yet
  versus resolved as annulled. Collapsing them looks harmless until
  something computes a scoring denominator over "resolved" forecasts.
- **Do not edit `resolutionCriteria` on a forecast that already has
  predictions.** It rewrites what the filed predictions were answering.
  Adding an anchor is safe because it does not change the question; changing
  the question is not. The repair is a new forecast.
- **A lint finding that cannot be honestly fixed should stay flagged.** F4
  has no external anchor in the world, and inventing a plausible URL would
  defeat the field's whole purpose. Two open findings that are documented
  beat a clean report that lies.
- **Put the rules where the test runner already looks.** Vitest includes
  `src/**/*.test.ts` only, so a pure module in `src/lib/` plus a thin CLI in
  `scripts/` gets real coverage where an all-in-`scripts/` version would get
  none.
- **Check the plan's counts before trusting them.** The plan said 19 edges;
  there are 25, because a domain landed in between. The conclusion held, but
  only by luck.
- **Parallel sessions cost nothing when the file partition is picked
  first.** Ten minutes of scope checking at the start produced two disjoint
  changesets and zero conflicts. Worktree isolation covered HEAD; the
  partition covered the files.
