## What happened

### 1. The close was about the line, not about aboard

The notice said remote servers move to `awesome-remote-mcp-servers` so the
old list "stays focused on local/stdio servers". Only seven pull requests
there were closed unmerged between 09-08 and 09-11, so this was per-PR
triage rather than a sweep, and the likeliest trigger is our own closing
clause, "or hosted at `https://aboard.untype.me/mcp`".

That list's `CONTRIBUTING.md` excludes servers that are *remote-only*.
aboard ships `aboard-mcp-server` on npm, so it qualifies, and 537 entries
there carry both the cloud and local markers. The two listings map onto
the connector-versus-server split Glama already draws: the package
belongs on the old list, the endpoint on the new one.

### 5. Bot Fight Mode, and why three sessions could not see it

Their CI reported the endpoint as needing a key, which their script only
does after a 401 or 403. The same request from this machine returned 200.
A probe from a GitHub Actions runner reproduced it: `HTTP/2 403`,
`cf-mitigated: challenge`, a "Just a moment..." interstitial. Telemetry
settled where the block sat: zero rows in the minute their CI ran,
against 18 in the surrounding forty minutes.

A zone-scoped token confirmed it at the source: `fight_mode: true` on a
Free plan, and 80 `botFight` managed challenges in 24 hours, 70 of them
on `/mcp`, from AWS, OVH, Hetzner, Google and Microsoft ASNs, with user
agents `node` (48), `python-httpx` and `mcp-watch/0.1`. The endpoint had
been refusing its own audience about seventy times a day since at least
2026-08-23.

Bot Fight Mode cannot be narrowed on this plan: Cloudflare documents that
WAF `Skip`, `Bypass` and `Allow` have no effect on it, and per-caller
exemptions need Super Bot Fight Mode. The operator turned it off in the
dashboard, the API write having been refused by this environment's
classifier. Nothing else changed.

Verified where it matters: from a runner, `initialize`, `tools/list` and
the site root all answer 200. Zero firewall challenges since. Worker
telemetry rose from roughly 27 to 60-74 probe arrivals an hour.

### 6. What is still not fixed

The Glama connector still reads Unhealthy roughly 26 hours after the fix,

check ruled out a second blocker: the zone's ~100 managed-rule blocks in
24 hours are vulnerability scans on `/`, `/admin` and `/wp-json/...`, with
none on `/mcp`.

## Decisions worth remembering

- **A rule only means something against the alternatives that exist.**
  "An unhealthy badge is worse than none" was sound where a badgeless
  entry could merge. On the new list it cannot, so the same rule would
  have meant not being listed. Re-read a standing rule against the venue
  before applying it.
- **"File and forget" is stale in both venues.** The old list merged 383
  PRs in one sweep on 09-07, median age 17 days, having merged 115 in all
  of August. Merges arrive in bursts that do reach recent submissions.
  Still do not sequence work behind them.
- **The Glama claim is now a gate, not a nicety.** Two maintainer
  conditions depend on it, one with a merge promise attached. It was
  demoted at the start of this session on the reasoning that the badge is
  uninformative. It is uninformative *and* required.
- **Instruments that sit downstream of the fault cannot see it.** The
  gate never calls production, `curl` from here is never challenged, and
  `wrangler tail` and `mcp_probe` observe only what arrives. The evidence
  was an absence of rows, and nothing prompts anyone to look for an
  absence. A vantage point outside the developer machine was available
  from 2026-08-23 and was not used for three sessions.
- **Verify a network fix from the network that was failing.** This
  machine's IP is never challenged and would have reported a false pass.
