## What happened

### 1. The draft contradicted the page it links

`research/launch-post-2026-08.md` told the F4 origin story as three
open-weights models converging at 0.40 to 0.42, a fourth returning 0.65,
and a spread of 0.25. F4 has held six predictions spanning 0.35 to 0.65
since 2026-07-18, and the site renders it plainly: `/claims/M4` says
"P=0.41 across 6 models, spread 0.30".

The dates are the part worth keeping. The sixth prediction landed a month
*before* the draft was written, so this was wrong when filed rather than
gone stale afterwards. The post's audience is people who check numbers,
the post links the page that refutes it, and the refutation is one click
from the claim.

The correction improves the argument rather than weakening it. The
convergence was an artifact of which three models had been asked, which
is a sharper version of the point the section exists to make.

A second error in the same family: the F7 paragraph credited both high
forecasters with extrapolating a 5-to-6 point annual rise. Only
`gpt-oss-120b` gives that reasoning. The 0.58 cites "ongoing development
and integration of automated decision-making systems" and runs no
arithmetic at all, which is now stated, because a forecaster showing no
work is evidence a reader should have.

## Decisions worth remembering

- **A nine-day-old draft is a source, not a fact.** The corpus moves, and
  prose that quotes it does not. The F4 error was a month old at the
  moment it was written, so no amount of "it was true when drafted" would
  have covered it. Anything citing `data/` gets re-checked against `data/`
  before it goes out.
- **Prefer the artifact the reader will check.** `/claims/M4` renders the
  spread the post got wrong. Verifying against the rendered page, not just
  the YAML, is what made the contradiction obvious rather than arguable.
- **A correction can strengthen the claim.** The true F4 story (apparent
  convergence dissolving as models were added) argues the post's thesis
  better than the version that was wrong. Fact-checking is not only
  damage control.
- **Do not ship a caveat-stripped citation in a post about rigor.** The
  ForecastBench parity headline without its 2024-vintage comparison data
  is exactly the move the post objects to elsewhere.
- **Name the limit you know about.** Review throughput was documented in
  `research/review-capacity.md` and absent from the post that creates the
  load. Saying it costs little; being found out by a contributor waiting
  in silence costs more.
- **Measured beats asserted, when the tool is one question away.** The
  telemetry sentence was going to ship as a hedge. It took one query to
  turn it into twelve days and 115 calls.
