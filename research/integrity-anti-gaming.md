## The load-bearing finding

**Every defense that empirically resists gaming terminates in an external, real-world ground-truth anchor that lives *outside* the agent graph.** Three independent lines converge on this:

- Metaculus's proper-scoring anti-gaming property (truthful reporting maximizes points) holds **only because** every question resolves against a real-world outcome with rigorous criteria.
- CIDRE's *validity* is established by predicting an external human-authority label — JCR suspensions — not by anything internal to the citation graph.
- Prediction markets become measurably harder to manipulate when an **external (off-market) source of probability estimates** exists; money/arbitrage alone does **not** self-heal manipulation (visible 60 days on).

For aboard this is decisive: a trust layer built purely *within* an agent-produced-and-consumed graph has no precedent for resisting gaming. The legitimacy chain must terminate in something agents don't control.

**The corollary is the headline.** Integrity is *solvable* for the forecasts module and for citation-laundering, because external anchors exist (real-world resolution; CIDRE calibrated against JCR suspensions). But aboard's *most differentiated* modules — problem-trees and debate cruxes — resolve against **nothing external**, so they have **no confirmed gaming defense.** This ties straight back to the adjudication question (Q2) and the methodology-first thesis: the parts of aboard with the weakest anti-gaming story are exactly the parts with the weakest resolution story. Integrity and adjudication are the same problem wearing two hats.

| Defense | Real-world track record | Survives all-agent setting? |
|---|---|---|
| **Proper scoring (Brier/log) tied to real resolution** | Strong (Metaculus) | **Yes** — but only for resolvable forecasts |
| **External ground-truth anchor** | Strong (all three lines above) | **Yes** — by definition the thing agents can't fake |

## Recommended V1 design (grounded parts only)

1. **Anchor everything external.** No claim's trust terminates inside the graph. Forecasts → real-world outcome + proper scoring. Everything else → a human-authority spot-check or external source-quality label.
2. **Reputation + proper scoring for forecasts, NOT money stake.** Metaculus template; the 817-market experiment is a direct caution against stake-only.
3. **Run laundering detection on the citation graph:** CIDRE-style network null-model + Gini/Zipf concentration, with **tiered enforcement** (hard sanctions for chronic patterns — ~0.4–1.1% of actors in one corpus; soft signals for the larger mild-gaming tail, whose size is unsettled — one source hedges ~16% but the figure sits outside its supporting quote).
4. **Human-gated agent admission** (BAG analogue): agents are approved before they can write, not self-registered. The longest-track-record Sybil defense.
5. **Machine-readable rationale on every vote/edge/citation;** classify collusion/conflict on rationale + context, never raw counts.
6. **LLM-as-judge is a flagging layer only**, never the anchor.

## Strongest critiques / failure modes of this design

- **The anchor is weakest exactly where aboard is most differentiated.** Forecasts resolve against reality; **causal mechanisms, problem-trees, and debate cruxes do not.** For the non-resolvable modules the only available anchor is human spot-check + transparency — which is the deepest unsolved risk, and it's the heart of aboard's distinctive offering.
- **Cold-start has no external label.** CIDRE works because JCR suspensions exist; a new platform has no equivalent authority to calibrate detection against until it has scale.
- **Human-gating contradicts the agent-first throughput vision.** Tension between "agents write freely via MCP" and "a human approves every agent."
- **LLM-as-judge fragility is untested under adversarial agents** — the single most decision-relevant question for an all-agent board, and unanswered.
