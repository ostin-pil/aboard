# aboard / agent-first landscape & methodology-thesis validation

Status: **draft — deep-research sync 2026-06-05.** Supplements `landscape.md` and `comparison-table.md` with a focused, agent-first re-read and an adversarial test of the methodology-first thesis. New 2026 evidence (Metaculus AIB, NeurIPS LLM-forecasting, WordLift RAG, Google ClaimReview retirement).

## How this was produced (and a caveat about confidence)

Generated by the `deep-research` workflow (5 search angles → 23 sources → 106 extracted claims → adversarial verification). **The verification phase broke structurally after the first 6 claims** — every subsequent verifier crashed without voting (logged as `0-0 abstain`, which means *not tested*, **not** *refuted*). So the raw report's headline ("forecasting validated; debate/crux/ semantic-publishing are the weak/risky bets") is largely an **artifact of a broken verifier**, not a finding. Only **3** claims were genuinely refuted; the rest of the non-forecasting material was never evaluated.

To correct this, the four thesis-critical precedents below were **re-verified by hand** (direct source fetches): Wikidata rank, AIF serialization, the WordLift schema.org-RAG effect sizes, and Google's 2025 ClaimReview retirement. Those are high-confidence. Treat everything else as descriptive-but-unverified.

## Verdict on the methodology-first thesis — three honest buckets

### ✅ Validated (real adversarial votes, primary sources)

- **An agent-first epistemic platform already exists, institutionally backed.** Metaculus runs an official **AI Benchmarking Tournament** (3 seasons/yr, ~$50k pool, 300–500 questions) with **first-party, MIT-licensed Python bot tooling**. Bots authenticate, fetch questions, and **write predictions back** via API (`post_binary_question_prediction(...)`) on GitHub-Actions cron — exactly the bidirectional read+write agent↔platform loop aboard envisions via JSON-LD/MCP. → *Implication:* the agent-first forecasting axis is **occupied**. Differentiation must come from the cross-module graph (problem-trees + debate + forecasts on one substrate), not from "agents forecast" alone. [forecasting-tools](https://github.com/Metaculus/forecasting-tools), [metac-bot-template](https://github.com/Metaculus/metac-bot-template), [AIB](https://www.metaculus.com/aib/)

- **Falsifiability rigor is enforced by centralizing the arbiter, not the crowd.** Metaculus: "Only Metaculus Administrators can resolve questions"; moderators "do not have authority to resolve"; resolution must come from "Publication from third-parties; or Discretion of Metaculus Admins"; and criteria must **avoid dependence on any individual/group "saying particular words or phrases."** → *Implication:* a direct template for aboard's resolution design — **and** a warning that **agent/crowd self-resolution has no precedent**. "Who resolves a contested agent-written claim?" is the core unsolved problem of the design. [question-checklist](https://www.metaculus.com/help/question-checklist/), [faq](https://www.metaculus.com/faq/)

- **Agents approach but do not beat the human crowd (as of 2024).** A news-retrieving LLM forecaster scored **Brier .179 vs the crowd's .149** and **71.5% vs 77.0%** on held-out binary questions (Halawi et al., UC Berkeley, NeurIPS 2024). → *Implication:* treat agent forecasts as **one calibrated input among many**, not ground truth — which empirically supports aboard's "don't-collapse-to-a-single-answer" instinct, *on the forecasting axis*. [arXiv 2402.18563](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18563v1)

- **Falsifiable resolved claims are already consumed as agent training/eval data, and curation is the bottleneck.** The same paper assembled **48,754 questions / 7.17M forecasts (2015–2024)** across 5 platforms, then had to **filter ~90% down to 5,516 well-defined binaries** ("ill-defined, overly personal, or niche"). → *Implication:* aboard's strongest wedge — **publish pre-curated, schema-validated, resolution-rigorous claims** and absorb the cleaning burden these researchers bear by hand.

### ❌ Genuinely refuted (real negative votes — caution against over-claiming)

These three were actually voted down. Note: they refute *strong claims in one 2025 preprint*, not aboard's design — but they are a direct caution against aboard marketing "structured argument = verifiable reasoning."

- **No verified cross-platform apples-to-apples scoring blueprint** exists in this set (Manifund matched-question methodology, **0-3**).
- **"Structured argumentation converts free-form LLM text into verifiable machine-readable reasoning chains"** did not survive (**0-2**, arXiv 2510.03442).
- **"Argument structure (not interpretability) is the right basis for trusting AI agents"** did not survive (**0-2**, same paper).

### 🔍 Found but untested this run → re-verified by hand (high confidence)

This is where the broken verifier did the most damage. Re-checked directly:

- **Both-readings-as-tension has a *deployed* precedent: Wikidata rank.** Statements are subject–predicate–object; every statement carries a rank (**preferred / normal / deprecated**) so **multiple competing values coexist** with signaled precedence rather than collapsing to one canonical value. This is not a novel-and-untested bet — it's a known, production data shape. [Wikidata:Data_model](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Data_model)

- **Machine-readable argument structure has ~20 years of precedent: AIF.** The Argument Interchange Format models arguments as **I-nodes** (claims/premises), **RA-nodes** (inference), **CA-nodes** (conflict/refutation), **PA-nodes** (preference) — and serializes to **RDFS (Semantic Web), JSON, Prolog, DOT/SVG**. → *Honest nuance:* precedent ≠ adoption. AIF dates to a **2005** colloquium and never reached mass use. The risk to aboard's argument-graph bet is **adoption, not feasibility**. [AIF (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argument_Interchange_Format)

- **JSON-LD-by-default is necessary but *not sufficient* for agent consumption.** WordLift's "Structured Linked Data as a Memory Layer for Agent-Orchestrated Retrieval" (2026) measured RAG accuracy: **bare Schema.org JSON-LD in `<script>` blocks gives only d=0.18 (small, +0.17)**; **dereferenceable, entity-centric pages** (NL summary + visible linked navigation + llms.txt-style agent instructions) give **+29.6%, d=0.60**; **agentic multi-step retrieval with link traversal** adds +0.50 accuracy (d=0.30) / +0.74 completeness (d=0.38). → *Implication:* aboard should not lean on JSON-LD markup alone. The payoff is in **dereferenceable entity pages + agent-navigable links + explicit agent instructions + multi-hop traversal**. This is the single most actionable design signal in the run. [arXiv 2603.10700](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10700v1)

- **Semantic-markup value can be platform-fragile (the contrarian signal).** In **June 2025 Google retired ClaimReview** fact-check snippets from Search/News — markup that had reached **~4B impressions/yr** across **250k+ tagged checks** over 10 years. Its value depended on **one platform's voluntary surfacing** and evaporated overnight. → *aboard's mitigation is structural:* serving agents **directly via its own API/MCP** does not depend on a third party choosing to surface markup — a positioning advantage worth naming. [Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/ifcn/2025/google-claimreview-fact-checks-snippets-removed/), [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2025/06/google-kills-the-fact-checking-snippet/)

**Net:** the forecasting/resolution half of the thesis is strongly validated and sits in *occupied* territory. The distinctive half (both-readings, cruxes, machine-readable argument) is **precedented but under-adopted** — the risk is adoption and the unsolved "who resolves" question, **not** that the ideas are unfounded. The earlier "riskiest bets" framing was a verifier artifact.

## Four-landscape synthesis (agent-first / UX / market lens)

| Landscape | How it handles falsifiable/contested claims | aboard's position |
|---|---|---|
| **Forecasting & markets** (Metaculus, Manifold, Polymarket, GJOpen, Kalshi, INFER) | Time-boxed questions, admin/third-party resolution, Brier/log scoring + calibration, community vs aggregate; **live bot/API ecosystems**. | **Redundant** on "agents forecast." **Differentiated** by binding forecasts into a cross-domain claim graph with mechanisms + debate. |
| **Argument mapping & debate** (Kialo, Arguman, DebateGraph, IBIS/Toulmin, Pol.is; AIF; CFAR double-crux) | Claims/premises + support/attack edges; cruxes; steel-manning. Rich theory (AIF), **chronic low adoption**. | **Differentiated** (dual-dossier + ranked cruxes as first-class data) but in the **lowest-adoption** category — de-risk via own working artifact. |
| **Knowledge graphs & semantic publishing** (Wikidata, schema.org/JSON-LD, ClaimReview, Hypothesis) | Subject–predicate–object statements; **Wikidata rank** holds multiple values; provenance/attribution; **markup adoption is platform-dependent and fragile**. | **Aligned** (JSON-LD-by-default, attribution). Adopt **rank-style both-readings**; avoid single-platform-surfacing dependence. |
| **AI-agent data & synthesis** (MCP ecosystem, Elicit, Consensus, Semantic Scholar/OpenAlex, ForecastBench, LLM-as-judge) | Agents need clean, dereferenceable, **navigable** structured claims; benchmarks demand contamination-free resolved questions. | **Strong fit + early demand.** A pre-curated, MCP-served falsifiable-claim graph has precedent (Metaculus AIB) and an unmet curation gap. |

## Prioritized, agent-first feature opportunities

Ranked by impact × evidence strength. Tagged **[agent]** where the primary consumer is a machine.

1. **First-party MCP server + agent template with API write-back** **[agent]** — read claims *and* submit claims/forecasts/cruxes back. Table-stakes per the Metaculus AIB precedent; aligns with the already-planned MCP work. *High impact.*
2. **Dereferenceable, entity-centric claim pages — not just JSON-LD markup** **[agent]** — NL summary + visible linked navigation between related claims + an `llms.txt`-style agent-instruction surface + multi-hop traversal. Evidence: bare markup = d=0.18 vs entity pages = +29.6% (WordLift). *High impact, evidence-backed.*
3. **Calibration & scoring per agent author** **[agent]** — Brier/log scores + calibration curves; surface where an agent's forecast diverges from the aggregate. Encodes "agent as one calibrated input, not ground truth." *High impact.*
4. **Machine-readable resolution-source field + a resolution-criteria lint** — structured `(third-party URL + concrete event/date)`; a validator that **rejects "X says Y" linguistic triggers** and flags ill-defined criteria. Straight from the Metaculus checklist; this is aboard's **quality wedge**. *High impact.*
5. **Pre-curated, schema-validated falsifiable-claims corpus as a product** — position the export as "clean agent training/eval data," absorbing the ~90% filtering that LLM-forecasting researchers do by hand. *Medium–high; positioning.*
6. **Wikidata-rank-style "both-readings" representation** — an explicit stance/rank field so multiple readings coexist with signaled (not collapsed) precedence. Gives the both-readings thesis a **precedented** data shape. *Medium.*
7. **Crux-graph as first-class typed edges** **[agent]** — the debate module's ranked cruxes as typed support/attack/preference edges (AIF-shaped). Build it as aboard's **own working artifact** (the de-risking move) rather than citing adoption that doesn't exist. *Medium; differentiation bet.*
8. **AIF / ClaimReview-compatible export (optional interop)** — do it for interoperability optionality, **not** as a growth engine: AIF has precedent without adoption, and ClaimReview's value proved platform-fragile. *Low; hedge.*

## Open questions (carried forward)

1. **Who resolves a contested claim in an agent-first graph?** Metaculus deliberately centralizes resolution in human admins and bans self/crowd resolution. aboard's "agents read *and* write" has **no verified precedent** for trustworthy agent/crowd self-resolution. This is the core unsolved problem.
2. **Argument structure: precedent without adoption.** AIF/IBIS/Kialo prove the shape is feasible but chronically under-adopted. Does aboard's agent-first audience change the adoption calculus, or inherit the same wall?
3. **Does JSON-LD-by-default actually help *agents*, or only crawlers?** WordLift says bare markup barely moves RAG; the win is entity pages + traversal. Is aboard building the high-leverage form or the low-leverage one?
4. **Does the 2024 "LLM nears but trails the crowd" result still hold in 2026,** and does aboard's curation measurably improve agent calibration vs raw platform data — i.e., is the curation advantage demonstrable, not just asserted?
